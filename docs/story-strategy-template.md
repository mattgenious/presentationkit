# Story Strategy Template

Use this before building a deck. The goal is to make the story choices explicit before layout work starts.

## Main objective

What should the audience believe, remember, or decide after the deck?

> Example: This team has turned a practical problem into a credible, guardrailed capability that can scale beyond the first use case.

## Narrative shape

Write the deck as a sequence of claims, not as a status report.

1. Context: why the problem space matters.
2. Proof: what exists today and why it is credible.
3. Ambition: what this unlocks next.

## Slide strategy

For each slide, define:

| Field | Prompt |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Why does this slide exist? |
| Main message | What single sentence should the audience retain? |
| On-slide copy | What minimal copy supports the message? |
| Visual role | What should the visual prove or clarify? |
| Aspect-ratio rule | What must not be stretched or compressed? |
| What to avoid | What would dilute the story? |
| Speaker intent | What should the presenter say out loud? |

## Visual rules

1. Do not place two detailed visuals on one slide unless both stay readable.
2. Use screenshots as evidence thumbnails unless they are genuinely readable at slide size.
3. Avoid repeated claims across title, subtitle, card labels, and captions.
4. Prefer one strong diagram over several competing diagrams.
5. Put guardrails near the capability they constrain, not as a disconnected legal footnote.

## Tone

Pick three to five words:

- Practical
- Credible
- Ambitious
- Evidence-first
- Responsible
